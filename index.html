Frequently Asked Questions

What is the optimal Carbon to Nitrogen (C:N) ratio for hot composting?
The biological sweet spot for rapid hot composting is **30:1** (30 parts carbon to 1 part nitrogen by weight). At 30:1, beneficial thermophilic bacteria have the exact balance of energy (carbon) and protein (nitrogen) needed to rapidly multiply and raise internal pile temperatures to 135°F–160°F, killing all weed seeds and pathogens in 3 to 5 days.
Why does my compost pile smell like ammonia or rotten eggs?
A foul-smelling compost pile means your C:N ratio is too low (excess nitrogen from too many kitchen scraps or fresh grass clippings) and the pile is anaerobic (lacking oxygen). Fix it immediately by turning the pile and mixing in 2 to 3 times more dry carbon 'browns' (dry autumn leaves, shredded cardboard, or clean wood shavings).
Are used coffee grounds considered a 'Brown' or a 'Green'?
Even though used coffee grounds are brown in color, chemically they are a powerful **'Green'** with an extremely rich C:N ratio of approximately 12:1 to 20:1. Coffee grounds provide abundant nitrogen that kick-starts intense heat inside microbial compost piles.
